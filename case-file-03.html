Field Report 03

Running a PMO Through a Corporate Carve-Out

I joined the engagement as Lead PMO shortly before the business unit entered a divestiture and began transitioning into a standalone company. Live projects still had to meet their schedules while the systems, reporting structures, and PMO processes supporting them were being transferred.

The starting problem

The carve-out did not pause project delivery. Finance still needed reliable forecasts and actuals, project owners still needed accurate status information, and leadership still needed a clear view of the portfolio. At the same time, project records, PMO documentation, and reporting processes had to move to the new organization. The main risk was losing reliable portfolio information during the transition.

What I owned

I led the PMO continuity and migration activities, with data integrity as the main priority. This included migrating all live project and portfolio records from Accolade to Planisware. I coordinated with project owners, finance, engineering, and account teams to validate the data, align the migration schedule, and resolve gaps before the transition. I also conducted user-training sessions to support adoption of the new platform.

Alongside the platform migration, I built and maintained the PMO Process Site in Confluence. I standardized the main templates, process documents, and governance material, and led their transfer to the new organization so the team retained a common reference after the carve-out.

Keeping the numbers honest

Financial reporting also had to remain reliable throughout the transition. I validated planned project expenses against the Annual Operating Plan and reconciled planned and actual expenditure with project owners and finance. I automated revenue reporting and developed Power BI dashboards covering revenue, margin, and spend. The resulting management dashboard supported decisions on project funding and prioritization.

A successful platform migration is not only a technical transfer. Project owners and leadership must still be able to trust the portfolio and financial data after the organization changes.

Where it landed

All live project and portfolio records were migrated from Accolade to Planisware without project disruption or continuity gaps. Structured training supported adoption of the new platform, while monthly NPI, financial MOS, and SKU reporting continued throughout the carve-out. Leadership retained a consistent view of project status and financial performance during the transition.

What I still use from this

A corporate transition increases the need for PMO discipline. Project data, financial reporting, and process documentation must remain reliable even while systems and reporting structures are changing. The experience reinforced that continuity depends on transferring not only the data, but also the processes, ownership, and knowledge required to use it.
