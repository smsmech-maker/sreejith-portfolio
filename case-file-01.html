Field Report 01

Establishing Common Gate Governance

I joined a Tier-1 automotive components manufacturer in January 2022 as Deputy Manager in its switch-systems division. More than 60 NPD projects were running concurrently, but engineering, quality, and supply chain used different definitions of project readiness. My priority was to establish common gate criteria and a consistent view of the portfolio.

The starting problem

Project status was being discussed through separate, informal updates rather than a shared governance process. Engineering could consider a project ready after design freeze while quality still viewed it as high risk because validation had not started. Supply chain was sometimes brought in too late. The teams needed one agreed definition of readiness at each stage.

What I built, in order

I started with the criteria rather than the software. I defined the evidence a project needed to move from concept to feasibility, design freeze, validation, and serial production. The framework used APQP and QCDDM (Quality, Cost, Delivery, Development, Management), which were already familiar to the teams.

I then introduced recurring gate-passage reviews. Projects could advance only when the required evidence was available. Some projects initially considered healthy did not clear the new criteria, which created resistance but also exposed risks that had previously remained inside subjective status updates.

Once the criteria and review rhythm were working, I introduced Zoho Projects as the shared system of record. This gave the teams one place to review gate status, actions, and project readiness.

The main challenge was not building the tracker. It was getting engineering, quality, and supply chain to agree on what a project had to demonstrate before it could advance.

Where it landed

Over that cycle the portfolio held a success rate above 85%, and around a dozen projects made it into serial production on plan. Catching cost issues at the gate instead of downstream also helped drive a high-single-digit margin improvement the following year. First Time Right — a decent proxy for how much rework the structure was preventing — moved from the low 60s to over 80%.

What I'd tell someone starting the same job

I would still define the governance process before selecting the tool. A dashboard can display project status, but it cannot resolve different interpretations of readiness. The useful work is agreeing on the evidence required at each gate and assigning clear ownership for it.
